Most days ask us to look quickly. We scan a room, a page, a face, and decide what matters before the details have had time to arrive. This is useful, right up until it becomes the only way we know how to see.

Slow looking is a small rebellion against that reflex. It is less about staring and more about staying: giving a place, an object, or an idea enough time to become specific.

## The first layer is rarely the whole story

Walk the same path twice and the second walk will not be the same. Light has moved. A branch is lower after the rain. Someone has left a chair near a window that was empty yesterday.

The changes are not dramatic, but drama is a poor measure of significance. The ordinary world is full of quiet evidence. Attention is how we learn to read it.

> To notice something is to let it become more than background.

I like to begin with a simple constraint: stay for five minutes longer than feels necessary. No camera at first. No attempt to turn the moment into a lesson. Just look until the obvious description runs out.

## A practice, not a personality

Some people seem naturally observant, but attention is less a gift than a habit. It can be trained in very modest ways:

- Describe one familiar object without naming it.
- Notice the furthest sound you can hear, then the nearest.
- Take the same photograph at the same hour for a week.
- Write down one detail that would be missing from a summary.

These exercises work because they interrupt recognition. Usually, once we know what a thing is, we stop seeing it. A tree becomes “tree.” A street becomes “the way home.” Naming helps us navigate, but it also closes the case.

Slow looking opens it again.

## Keep what you notice

A notebook makes attention cumulative. A line or two is enough: the color of the sky before rain, a good hinge on an old cabinet, the way a stranger explained something clearly. Alone, each note is small. Together, they become a record of what your mind reaches for.

The aim is not to be endlessly mindful. It is simply to remain available to surprise. The world is generous with detail when we give it somewhere to land.
